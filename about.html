— about the artist —

A small studio, and a longer silence.

Akshada

Akshada is a painter working out of a small north-facing room in Pune. She has been making devotional work — quietly, and only as often as the work asks — for the last eight years.

Her practice began with a single canvas of Adiyogi, painted over the course of one monsoon. It has since grown into six ongoing series, all returning to the same question: what does it mean to sit with a form long enough that it begins to look back?

Process

Each work is hand-painted on cotton or hand-prepared board. Pigments are ground in the studio, often from mineral sources — lapis, malachite, sindoor, oxidised silver. Gold leaf is laid by hand, never applied as foil.

No edition is ever larger than nine. Most originals are one of one.
